# HBS Online — Key Frameworks

## 1. Management Essentials

### Module 1: A Process Perspective on Management

- **Management defined:** Coordinated action in pursuit of performance (coordination + action + performance)
- **Tactical choices as authority:** Who to invite to meetings, who to assign tasks to, how to delegate, how to lead post-mortems, how to give feedback, how to get buy-in, how to sequence conversations
- **Three organizing tools:** Strategy, People, Processes
- **Process design:** Every process is perfectly designed to create its outputs. Bad output = flawed process.
- **Management Process Model:** Ideas/Intentions/Goals/Targets --> [multiple parallel process paths] --> Results/Performance/Accomplishments

**Managing vs Leading:**

| Dimension | Management | Leadership |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Purpose | Dealing with complexity | Dealing with change |
| Focus | Execution, "doing things right" | Change, innovation, "doing the right things" |
| Main Skills | Planning, organizing, monitoring | Formulating visions, aligning, motivating |
| Orientation | Present, short-term | Future, long-term |
| Seeks | Order, consistency, efficiency | Change, newness, effectiveness, movement |

### Module 2: Shaping the Decision-Making Process

- **Decision quality:** Good decisions have Quality + Executability + Timeliness
- **Decision methods:** Consensus, Devil's Advocacy, Dialectical Inquiry
- **Inquiry vs Advocacy:** Inquiry = collaborative problem-solving, testing assumptions, collective ownership. Advocacy = contest, persuasion, winners/losers.
- **Cognitive vs Affective conflict:** Cognitive (task-focused) is constructive. Affective (personal) is destructive.
- **Groupthink symptoms:** Invulnerability, rationalization, self-censorship, mindguards, stereotyped views, direct pressure on dissenters
- **3 C's of decision-making:** Constructive Conflict, Consideration (fairness of process), Closure (alignment, not unanimity, on a timeline)

**Techniques for increasing cognitive conflict while reducing affective conflict:**
1. Require vigorous debate — ask tough questions, pose unexpected hypotheticals
2. Prohibit language that triggers defensiveness — preface with "let me play devil's advocate"
3. Break up natural coalitions — assign people across traditional loyalties
4. Shift individuals out of grooves — ask people to argue positions they didn't endorse
5. When stalemated, revisit key information and underlying assumptions

**Two formal debate structures:**
- **Point-Counterpoint:** Split into subgroups, each develops proposals, then debate and seek agreement on shared assumptions before developing common recommendations
- **Intellectual Watchdog:** Subgroup A develops proposal, Subgroup B critiques assumptions and recommendations, iterate revision-critique cycles until convergence

**Consideration as procedural justice:** Voice alone isn't enough — people must believe the leader actively listened and weighed their views. Demonstrate by: conveying openness, not disclosing preferences early, listening attentively, absenting yourself from early deliberations, explaining rationale after deciding.

**Closure — two failure modes:**
- **Deciding too early:** Groupthink, desire to be team players overrides critical thinking. Watch for body language of latent discontent.
- **Deciding too late:** Gridlock, analysis paralysis, demand for certainty. Leader must "call the question."

**Litmus test (5 process traits for superior outcomes):**
1. Multiple genuine alternatives being considered?
2. "Facts" being carefully tested or merely asserted?
3. Goals specified up front and stable?
4. Contrarian questions being asked? Healthy listening?
5. Participation maintained after key milestones?

### Module 3: Implementation and Learning

**Seven-step Implementation Model:**
1. **Set goals and define deliverables** — articulate, communicate, reiterate goals at every chance
2. **Determine roles, responsibilities, and relationships** — many can be responsible but only one accountable; appoint single authority; lay out reporting/dependencies/communication up front
3. **Delegate the work** — don't fumble handoff, don't skip essentials (resources, authority, context), don't delegate without confidence/trust, don't overload people
4. **Execute, monitor, and provide support** — iterate, maintain options, set up early warning systems, build short feedback loops, give critical feedback with psychological safety, don't monitor without providing support
5. **Take corrective action** — communicate problems early (they don't age well), make minor adjustments daily, don't make minor adjustments when major revisions are needed, don't point fingers (most problems are systemic)
6. **Get closure** — define "done," keep stakeholders in loop, recognize contributions
7. **Conduct retrospective** — clarify what you set out to do vs. what happened, discuss what went right AND what to sustain, document lessons, create action plans, provide clear individual feedback, get feedback on your own performance

**The Learning Process Model:** Acquire --> Interpret --> Apply

**Acquiring information hurdles:**
- Blind spots (confirmation bias), Filtering (selectively taking in data), Low signal-to-noise ratios, Lack of information sharing

**Interpreting information — cognitive biases:**
- Availability bias, Illusion of validity, Illusory causation, Hindsight bias

**Applying information hurdles:**
- Unclear implications, Complacency, Bad habits, Risk aversion, Poor self-awareness

**Four conditions for learning to flourish:**
1. Recognition and acceptance of diverse perspectives
2. Provision of timely feedback (compress learning cycles, use prototypes)
3. Pursuit of new ways of thinking (focus externally, "steal ideas shamelessly")
4. Acceptance of errors and mistakes (psychological safety)

**Feedback framework:**
- Give informally and regularly, not just annual reviews
- Base on facts/observation, not impressions
- Focus on performance, not personality — make it a conversation, not a speech
- Make specific and actionable ("Great job!" is not useful)
- Help with follow-through: action plan, support, check-ins

## 2. Negotiation Mastery

### The Central Tension
- **Competition vs. Cooperation:** You want a good deal, but agreement requires finding outcomes that work for both sides. Eliciting cooperative behavior from your counterpart to advance your own interests is the central task.
- **Reciprocity risk:** Aggressive moves trigger retaliation. Collaborative gestures may be reciprocated — but can also be misread as weakness.

### Module 1: Negotiation Analysis — Finding the ZOPA

- **Four phases:** Preparation --> Bargaining --> Closing --> Learning from experience
- **BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement):** Your best option if negotiation fails. Always strive to improve it. Estimate counterpart's BATNA but test assumptions — circumstances can change mid-negotiation.
- **WALKAWAY:** Your minimum acceptable deal. Influenced by BATNA but not identical (e.g., you might discount for a friend). Calculate tentatively before negotiating; adjust based on what you learn.
- **ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement):** Area between each party's walkaway. Can be large or small. You'll only know your own walkaway for certain.
- **Stretch Goal:** Best-case scenario with ~1-in-10 chance. Low aspirations are self-fulfilling.
- **First Offers & Anchoring:** If confident about ZOPA boundaries, go first to anchor. If unclear, wait. Results tend to converge between the first two numbers.
- **Information is power** — that's why people hide BATNAs and WALKAWAYs.

### Module 2: Advanced Analysis — Creating Value

- **Value creation = finding uncommon ground.** Value comes from *differences* between parties:
  - **Valuation differences:** Parties value same item differently based on need/resources
  - **Time horizon differences:** One needs cash now, other can wait (installment structures)
  - **Expectation differences:** Pessimist selling to optimist (contingency clauses)
  - **Risk tolerance differences:** Same expectations, different willingness to bear risk
- **Creating vs. Claiming value tension:** Value creation requires understanding priorities, but disclosure risks exploitation. Central dilemma of multi-issue negotiation.
- **2D ZOPA mapping:** In multi-issue transactions, ZOPA is an *area* (not a line). Reveals suboptimal deals where both could improve.
- **Four barriers to agreement:**
  1. Lack of creativity (zero-sum mindset is self-fulfilling)
  2. Tactical miscalculation (bold anchoring can backfire)
  3. Poor process management (resolving issues one-at-a-time creates win-lose series; consider packages of 2+ issues simultaneously)
  4. Relationship problems (distrust hampers value creation)
- **Preparing for value creation:**
  - Weigh your tradeoffs — identify, rank priorities
  - Establish a baseline — formulate barely acceptable package + two alternative packages of equal value
  - Set a stretch goal — plausible best-case + two equally good alternatives
  - Consider counterpart's likely priorities — test and refine assumptions as negotiation unfolds

### Module 3: Managing the Process — Bargaining Style & Emotion

- **Substantive vs. Relational dimensions:** How parties relate directly impacts value generation and whether agreement is reached.
- **Three levels master negotiators monitor:**
  - **Engaging (WHO):** Who's at the table and how they relate
  - **Framing (WHAT):** What the negotiation is about and how issues are defined
  - **Norming (HOW):** Behavioral norms and ground rules
- **Tactics — reject rigid rules.** Context matters. Strategy drives tactics. Every action should advance your overall plan.
- **Emotion:** Use the 6-step Emotional Preparation exercise. Anxiety is common — don't confirm defensive counterparts' fears.
- **Strategic and Relational Agility model (four axes):**
  - Creating Value (top) vs. Claiming Value (bottom)
  - Assert (left) vs. Empathy (right)
  - Agility — not a fixed style — determines success across situations.

### Module 4: Forging Agreement Within an Organization

- **Internal negotiations are often the hardest:** Can't walk away from colleagues. Same principles apply, but context changes everything.
- **Deal fatigue:** Risk of burnout in prolonged negotiations. Manage expectations of internal stakeholders throughout.
- **Internal support for implementation:** External deals fail without sufficient internal buy-in.
- **Practical conjoint analysis for multi-issue negotiations:**
  1. Identify minimally acceptable comprehensive deal
  2. Imagine two alternative packages of equal value (baseline/floor)
  3. Do the same for three excellent outcomes of equal value (stretch/ceiling)
- **Managing others' emotions:**
  - Emotional intelligence (Voss): Recognizing what others are really expressing
  - Redirecting upset people (Hoffman): Help them think clearly and act in their self-interest
  - Anxiety reframing (Brooks): Transform anxiety to excitement — "I am excited!"
- **Cross-cultural issues:** Don't overgeneralize by nationality. Culture includes professional background. Often bigger differences within groups than between them.

### The Learning System

- **Core principle:** Focus on process (within your control), not outcomes (not fully in your control).
- **Preparation checklist (7 items):** (1) Identify interests and tradeoffs, (2) Craft stretch goal, (3) Calculate provisional walkaway, (4) Prepare for best/worst case, (5) Estimate counterpart's priorities and walkaway, (6) Sharpen persuasion ("to get a yes, expect a no"), (7) Recognize what you don't know
- **Write down your plan.** Rough notes for simple negotiations, detailed documents for important ones. Get feedback from a colleague.
- **After-action review (6 steps):** (1) What did you anticipate correctly vs. should have foreseen? (2) Two things that worked well. (3) Two things you'd do differently. (4) Check with colleague for missed insights. (5) Develop personal catalog of best practices. (6) Consult it every time you negotiate.
- **Guiding maxim:** Never be the least prepared person at the bargaining table.

## 3. Power & Influence

### Module 1: Understanding the Nature of Power

**Three misconceptions about power:**
1. "Power is dirty" — Truth: nothing intrinsically dirty; necessary for positive change
2. "Power is something you possess" — Truth: power is a force within relationships, not a personal attribute
3. "Power is only for those at the top" — Truth: authority can give power but doesn't guarantee it; low rank is not a barrier

**The 4-Element Power Model:**
1. Resources they value (to which you have access) — YOUR POWER
2. Their alternatives (to getting what they want elsewhere) — YOUR POWER
3. Resources you value (to which they have access) — THEIR POWER
4. Your alternatives (to getting what you want elsewhere) — THEIR POWER

**Four strategies to shift the balance:**
1. **Attraction** — Increase the value of your resources in their eyes
2. **Consolidation** — Decrease their alternatives to you
3. **Expansion** — Increase your alternatives to them
4. **Withdrawal** — Decrease your interest in their resources

**Power poisons & antidotes:**
- Hubris --> Humility (awareness of impermanence, accurate self-perception)
- Self-focus --> Empathy (awareness of interdependence)
- Together lead to Higher Purpose (power as responsibility)

**Three sources of power:** Positional (formal roles), Personal (individual attributes, context-dependent), Relational (your relationships)

**Power mapping:** Observe who visits whom, who defers, who commands attention. Engage people one-on-one to understand what they want, then find ways to control access to those resources.

### Module 2: Building Relational Sources of Power

**Two key questions:** (1) What do people value? (2) Who controls access to what people value?

**Network centrality:** Being an "advice hub" (people come to you) or a "bridge" (connecting disconnected groups). Informal networks often don't reflect formal hierarchies.

**Three network characteristics:**
- **Breadth** — Diversity of backgrounds and perspectives
- **Depth** — Strength of relationships (strong ties = trust/loyalty; weak ties = novel information/creativity)
- **Density** — How interconnected your contacts are (high = trust/coordination; low = non-redundant info/brokerage)

**Homophily:** Tendency to bond with similar people. Leads to limited information, confirmation bias, poor power-mapping.

**Building network diversity:** Empathic inquiry (genuine curiosity about others' perspectives) + Multifaceted disclosure (sharing aspects that make you different)

**Networking mindset:** Shift from prevention focus (obligation to avoid negatives) to promotion focus (opportunity to learn/grow). Four ways: focus on learning, identify common interests, think broadly about what you can give, find a higher purpose.

### Module 3: Earning Trust and Exercising Influence

**Six resources people value:** Material resources, Status, Autonomy, Affiliation, Achievement, Morality — all in service of Safety and Self-esteem.

**Trust = Warmth + Competence.** When forced to choose, people prefer warmth.
- High competence + High warmth = **Lovable Star** (desperately wanted)
- High competence + Low warmth = **Competent Jerk** (mostly avoided)
- Low competence + High warmth = **Lovable Fool** (mildly wanted)
- Low competence + Low warmth = **Incompetent Jerk** (desperately avoided)

**Reducing bias:** (1) Alter the images you're exposed to, (2) Have positive equal-status contact with outgroups, (3) Confront discrimination when you see it

**Situational Influence Model (5 styles):**
- **Push — Persuading:** Logic/reasoning. Best when you have facts/data and are respected as expert.
- **Push — Asserting:** Stating expectations, using incentives/pressure. Best when quick compliance needed and you have authority.
- **Pull — Bridging:** Listening, involving, exchanging information. Best when you need long-term commitment and value their input.
- **Pull — Attracting:** Finding common ground, sharing vision. Best when you want to generate energy and share common values.
- **Move Away — Disengaging:** Postpone, take a break. Best when tension is unmanageable or new facts arise.

No single style is always best. Analyze the situation first.

**Three Difficult Conversations (Stone, Patton, Heen):**
1. **"What Happened"** — Don't present perceptions as absolute truth. Describe your perceptions, acknowledge your own role, describe impact on you.
2. **"Feelings"** — Don't avoid or vent. Address feelings without judgments or attributions.
3. **"Identity"** — Don't self-blame or protect self-image at all costs. Understand identity issues at stake for each party.

### Module 4: Encountering Entrenched Power

**Power hierarchies** persist through: obedience to authority, psychological impact on both groups, and legitimizing myths (stories that make the status quo seem natural).

**The Infernal Trio (psychology of the powerful):**
1. Lack of empathy — easier to hoard resources
2. Enhanced sense of agency — more prone to acting on affronts
3. Tendency to view own actions as legitimate — even when unethical

**Three levels of intervention:** Individual (alter images, seek outgroup contact, confront discrimination), Cultural (reshape informal norms), Institutional (change policies and practices)

### Module 5: Leading Change

**Why change is difficult:** Entrenched hierarchies are taken for granted, people may not see change as in their interest, change triggers primitive safety signals, resistance is often covert.

**Assessing environment readiness (3 questions):**
1. Have there been recent crises or jolts?
2. Is there a single dominant model/approach?
3. How deeply ingrained are existing practices?

**Divergent vs. Nondivergent changes:** Divergent breaks with norms (harder). Nondivergent builds on existing practices (easier).

**Stakeholder Analysis:**
1. Identify stakeholders (who is affected, who cares, who contributes, who gives legitimacy)
2. Categorize: Endorsers, Resisters, Fence-sitters, Not Enough Data
3. Prioritize influential stakeholders (thought leaders, advice hubs, bridges)

**Engagement by type:**
- Endorsers: Nurture support, give them co-ownership work
- Fence-sitters: Get close to them — trust is key, closeness significantly helps
- Resisters (nondivergent change): Get close — social obligation may cause rethinking. (Divergent change): Engage with caution — closeness can be a liability

**Three leadership roles for change:**
- **Agitator** — Highlights the problem, launches collective action. Trap: fragmented agitation, stalled solution.
- **Innovator** — Creates actionable solutions. Trap: blindness to negative implications, impractical elegance.
- **Orchestrator** — Coordinates across groups to scale adoption. Trap: mission drift and dilution.

Playing all three at once is challenging — team up with others.

### Module 6: Managing Your Relationship to Power

**Cultivating empathy (antidote to self-focus):**
- Experience other people's jobs literally
- Use storytelling to make things personal ("safe talk space")
- Embed interdependence in systems ("How did you contribute to someone else's impact?")
- Step outside your organization into the "real world"

**Cultivating humility (antidote to hubris):**
- Make it desirable to say "I don't know"
- Establish channels for honest input (build a "council")
- Create visible signs that success is fleeting
- Assess and reward humility in leaders

**Two levers for keeping power in check:**
1. **Sharing power** — Not zero-sum; sharing means rebalancing. Structures: cooperatives, codetermination (workers on boards).
2. **Holding the powerful accountable** — Shift narrative from single "face" to all employees. Create boards as counterweight.

## 4. Strategy Execution

### Module 1: Managing the Tensions of Strategy Execution

- **Why strategies fail:** Reluctance to make tough choices about opportunities, customers, locations. Without focus: too many initiatives, poor communication, ineffective resource distribution.
- **Core tensions:** Growth vs. Profit vs. Control. Short-term vs. Long-term. Unlimited opportunities vs. Limited attention. Employee motivations vs. Organizational blocks.
- **Return on Management:** Productive organizational energy released / Management time and attention invested.
- **Fundamental tension:** Innovation vs. Control. Find the right balance for value creation.

**Employee Motives & Organizational Blocks:**
- Want to **contribute** — but orgs fail to communicate strategy so employees don't see how they fit
- Want to **do right** — but pressure + access to assets tempts rule-bending; no clear boundaries
- Want to **achieve** — but lack resources or face too many competing demands
- Want to **innovate** — but lack resources or fear challenging the status quo

**Levers of Control (core framework):**
1. **Beliefs Systems** — Mission/credos/core values. What people *should do*. Unleashes desire to contribute.
2. **Boundary Systems** — Codes of conduct. What people *should not do*. Allows people to do right.
3. **Diagnostic Control Systems** — Monitor outcomes, correct deviations. Unleashes desire to achieve.
4. **Interactive Control Systems** — Focus attention on emerging threats/opportunities. Unleashes desire to innovate.

**Four Ps of Strategy (maps to Levers):**
1. **Perspective** (mission/purpose) --> Beliefs Systems --> Core Values
2. **Position** (competitive value prop) --> Boundary Systems --> Risks to Avoid
3. **Plans** (specific goals/measures) --> Diagnostic Control Systems --> Critical Performance Variables
4. **Patterns of Action** (detect/adapt to landscape) --> Interactive Control Systems --> Strategic Uncertainties

### Module 2: Aligning Job Design to Strategy

**Five common business strategies:** Low-Cost (Walmart), Local Value Creation (Nestle), Global Standard of Excellence (L'Oreal), Dedicated Service Relationship (IBM), Expert Knowledge (Google/Merck)

**Job Design Spans (four spans):**
1. **Span of Control** — Resources at your disposal (people, budgets, decision rights)
2. **Span of Accountability** — Performance measures used to evaluate you
3. **Span of Influence** — Time/attention needed to influence others
4. **Span of Support** — Support you can expect from people in other units

- Hard spans: Control + Accountability (formal). Soft spans: Influence + Support (cultural).
- **Supply = Demand:** (Control + Support) must equal (Accountability + Influence)
- **Entrepreneurial Gap:** When Accountability > Control, people must behave entrepreneurially. Intentional design choice.

**Design levers for each span:**
- Control: Add/remove resources, headcount, budget, decision rights
- Accountability: Widen (fewer measures = more entrepreneurship) or Narrow (more measures = more compliance)
- Influence: Widen via entrepreneurial gap, stretch goals, cross-unit allocation, matrix reporting
- Support: Build shared purpose, group identity, trust, equity-based rewards

### Module 3: Energizing Employees to Execute Strategy

**Six techniques for generating creative tension:**
1. **Entrepreneurial Gap** — Accountability > Control. Forces cross-unit interaction. Risk: failure without support.
2. **Stretch Goals** — Force employees to work differently, not just harder. Risk: mistakes increase. Requires safe learning environment.
3. **Ranking Systems** — Forced distribution. Creates healthy competition. Risk: stress, unhealthy competition.
4. **Cross-Unit Teams** — Promotes innovation and information sharing. Risk: slower decisions.
5. **Solid/Dotted-Line Reporting** — Two bosses break down silos.
6. **Matrix Organizations** — Both bosses have equal authority. Forces negotiation. Risk: tension, slow coordination.

- **Goal difficulty sweet spot:** Motivating but not perceived as impossible. Too easy = complacency. Too hard = giving up.
- **Don't overuse techniques:** Too many at once creates bureaucratic, slow-moving organizations.

**Beliefs Systems & Core Values:**
- In small companies, values are informal. As companies grow, formalize in credos/missions.
- Most effective when they clarify whose interests to prioritize in difficult decisions.
- **Value Creation Theory — Who to prioritize:** Customer satisfaction --> Customers. Unique employee talents --> Employees. Cash flow/ROI --> Shareholders.

### Module 4: Measuring and Monitoring Performance

- **Diagnostic Control Systems** implement Strategy as Plans. Management by Exception: pre-set goals, only alert on deviations. Frees management attention.
- **Profit Plans:** Primary diagnostic system. Three questions: (1) Enough profit to cover costs and reinvest? (2) Enough cash to remain solvent? (3) Sufficient returns for investors?

**Three Financial Wheels:**
1. **Profit Wheel:** Sales --> Operating Expenses --> Investment --> Profits
2. **Cash Wheel:** Operating Cash --> Inventory --> Sales --> A/R. Move cash through as quickly as possible.
3. **ROE Wheel:** Profits --> Equity --> ROE --> Asset Utilization. ROCE = (Net Income / Sales) x (Sales / Capital Employed).

**Balanced Scorecard (four perspectives, bottom-up):**
1. **Learning & Growth** — Human capital, information capital, organization capital
2. **Internal Business Process** — Innovation, quality, operations, supplier management
3. **Customer** — NPS, repeat purchases, brand image, relationships
4. **Financial** — Revenue, margins, cash flow

- **Strategy Map:** Define cause-and-effect relationships across perspectives. The arrows are the most important part — they reveal the theory of value creation.
- **Magic Number Seven:** Managers should be accountable for no more than 7 (+-2) measures. Delegate the rest.
- **Critical Performance Variables:** Factors that would cause strategy to fail if not achieved.

**Three tests for effective measures:**
1. Does the measure align with strategy?
2. Is it objective, complete, and responsive?
3. Does it link to economic value?

**Incentive Design:** Bonus pool size (function of financial performance), Allocation basis (individual vs. unit vs. corporate), Formula vs. subjective judgment. Beyond cash: gifts, feedback, growth opportunities, recognition, stock options tied to performance.

### Module 5: Identifying and Managing Risks

**Four types of strategic risk (core outward):**
1. **Operations Risk** — Operational errors (defective products, system failures, shipping errors)
2. **Asset Impairment Risk** — Financial (credit, currency), IP (patent infringement, trade secrets), Physical (disasters, accidents)
3. **Competitive Risk** — Changes in competitive environment. Analyze with Porter's Five Forces.
4. **Franchise Risk** — When any risk threatens the entire business through loss of confidence by critical constituents

**Internal Pressure Points (Risk Exposure Calculator):** Rate 1-5 on each:
- Growth pressures: performance expectations, expansion rate, employee inexperience
- Culture pressures: entrepreneurial risk rewards, executive resistance to bad news, internal competition
- Information pressures: transaction complexity, diagnostic measure gaps, decentralized decisions
- Scores: 9-20 Safety Zone, 21-34 Caution Zone, 35-45 Danger Zone

**The Dangerous Triad (enabling misconduct):**
1. **Pressure** — Under pressure to achieve at all costs
2. **Opportunity** — Discover they can break rules
3. **Rationalization** — Explain away their breach of trust
- Remove at least one leg.

**Internal Controls (reduce Opportunity):** Structural safeguards (segregation of duties, audits), Systems safeguards (documentation, audit trails), Staff safeguards (training, rotation in key jobs)

**Business Conduct Boundaries (reduce Rationalization):** Clear list of prohibited actions, stated in the negative, explain *why*, concise enough to remember, clear sanctions.

### Module 6: Balancing Innovation and Control

**Strategic Boundaries:** Define which opportunities employees should *not* pursue. Maintain focus. Must be continually reassessed so they don't prevent adaptation.

**Intended vs. Emergent vs. Realized Strategies:**
- Intended = planned in advance, implemented via beliefs/boundary/diagnostic systems
- Emergent = arise spontaneously in response to unforeseen threats/opportunities
- Realized = Intended + Emergent. Both are necessary.

**Interactive Control Systems:** An *existing* control system managers choose to use interactively (for debate/dialogue, not just monitoring). Focus disproportionate attention on it. Facilitate bottom-up information flow. Top-down questions flow down; learning, tactics, actions flow up.

**Strategic Uncertainties (what to track):** Assumptions about customers, competition, and capabilities that underpin strategy. If false, strategy collapses. Unknowable in advance.

**Critical Performance Variables vs. Strategic Uncertainties:**
- CPVs: "What must we do well to achieve intended strategy?" --> efficiency/effectiveness
- SUs: "What changes could alter our vision?" --> disruptive change, driven by top management unease

**Requirements for effective interactive control systems:**
1. Simple to understand
2. Useful to managers at multiple levels
3. Provide info about strategic uncertainties
4. Trigger debate leading to revised action plans (What changed? Why? What do we do?)

**Complete Levers of Control Model:**
- Beliefs Systems inspire --> unleash desire to **contribute**
- Boundary Systems limit rationalization --> allow people to **do right**
- Diagnostic Systems provide direction/resources --> unleash desire to **achieve**
- Interactive Systems foster learning --> unleash desire to **innovate**
